\title{Equilibrium from the inside out:\\ The relativistic structure of Quantum Thermodynamics}
\author{Gerard McCaul}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
We obtain the exact Hamiltonian of mean force for a finite-dimensional quantum
system at arbitrary coupling to a Gaussian environment - non-perturbative,
closed-form, and valid at all temperatures. The key is to trace the bath
\emph{before} expanding: exactness then follows from the boundedness of the
system algebra, not from resumming a divergent series. For a qubit with
non-commuting coupling, the solution exposes a hidden geometric structure. The
bath breaks the orientability of the influence state, forcing the reduced
equilibrium into one of two dual representations related by an involutive map.
In natural thermal coordinates this map is a Lorentz boost: a fixed sum of
rapidities, rectangular hyperbolas, a light cone, a Minkowski metric. The
crossover between weak and strong coupling is governed by a single universal
function that simultaneously maximises the extractable quantum work, and the
ultrastrong limit of Cresser and Anders emerges as the zero-coupling
asymptotic of the dual frame. For $N$-level systems the metric generalises:
the spectrum of transition frequencies couples to the physical temperature
through a renormalisation tensor, and the passage from special to general
relativity is immediate.
\end{abstract}
