\title{Dual Representatives of Mean-Force Equilibrium as a Duality Geometry}
\author{Author Name}
\affiliation{Affiliation}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
The Hamiltonian of mean force is exact but often difficult to use in practice because low-temperature
and strong-coupling limits are not equally tractable in one parameter representation.
We present a physical duality-geometry view in which two discrete representative maps reorganize this
difficulty without changing the underlying state family.
The first map is a strong-weak reflection of the crossover coordinate \(y=\log\chi\), yielding
\(\chi\mapsto\chi^{-1}\) and, for one-channel scaling, \(g^\vee=g_\star^2/g\).
The second map is an orientation-gauge flip of the mean-force axis that induces an affine reflection
of effective-temperature representatives, \(\beff'=2\beta-\beff\).
In the main text we emphasize physical interpretation, concrete control logic, and observable
consequences; formal theorem-level statements and full proofs are collected in the Appendix.
The spin-boson qubit is used as a first worked example after the general framework is established,
including closure-level spectral-class thresholds and a closed entropy map.
Numerical diagnostics confirm that asymptotic reconstructions are inaccurate only near crossover and
rapidly become quantitatively precise deep in the target branch.
\end{abstract}
