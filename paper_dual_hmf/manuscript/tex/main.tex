\title{Dual Representatives of Mean-Force Equilibrium}
\author{Author Name}
\affiliation{Affiliation}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
We formulate a theory of dual representatives for mean-force equilibrium in which asymptotic control
is exchanged by discrete transformations on representative labels. The first transformation is a
strong-weak involution acting on the crossover coordinate \(y=\log\chi\), yielding
\(\chi\mapsto\chi^{-1}\) and, in single-channel scaling, \(g^\vee=g_\star^2/g\). The second is an
\(Z_2\) orientation gauge on the mean-force eigenaxis, inducing an affine reflection on effective
temperature representatives, \(\beff'=2\beta-\beff\). We prove the involutive and affine identities,
state the admissible observable class for asymptotic exchange, and derive representative constraints
for effective-temperature parameterisations. Only after the formal theory is established do we
instantiate it in a spin-boson qubit as Example~1, where the \(g\to\infty\) sector is analytically
easy in the influence-operator representation \(e^S\), and is used to access hard low-temperature
control through the dual map. We conclude with a minimal validation protocol centered on a single
\(y=\log\chi\) figure and identity checks.
\end{abstract}
